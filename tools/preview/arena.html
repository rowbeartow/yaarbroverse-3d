How a rope swing goes

  • Taking hold. A rope hangs still from a bough on a bare snag, out past the edge, its end a jump’s height up. Jump at it and the hero grabs it, with no button.
  • Swinging. The hero swings out and back, always the same width of swing, for as long as they hold on. The stick does nothing. One swing out and back takes about three seconds.
  • Letting go. Jump lets go. The hero flies on the way the rope was going, with a small kick upward, and can steer in the air. Let go on the way forward to fly on. Let go at the back of the swing and the hero drops short.
  • A miss lands in the cove, never a fall. Swim back and climb onto the rock island or the rock stack.
  • The same rope goes in any level: a level hangs one by saying where it is tied, how long it is, which way it carries and what it leads to.

On each difficulty

  • Easy: letting go at any point in the swing, forward or back, throws the hero onto the rock stack or the ledge. The stick cannot spoil it. The rope is also a little easier to reach.
  • Normal: let go anywhere on the way forward and you get there. The stack is 6 m across.
  • Hard: the stack is 4.4 m across and the ledge is further off, so fewer moments work: let go in the upper half of the way forward.
  • The bot crosses on every difficulty as each hero. It holds on until letting go would land it.

How the crocodile stepping stones go

  • Lying low. A row of big crocodiles lies across the channel with only eyes and snouts showing. They are not there to stand on.
  • Surfacing. As the hero comes near they come up, the nearest first, backs awash. A back is 3 m long and 1.2 m across.
  • Holding. From the first step on one it holds for a while, then goes under. For the last moment it shudders and growls: its warning. Hopping off does not reset it.
  • Going under drops the hero in the water, never a fall. Nobody can climb onto a crocodile from the water: swim to the rock at the start, the rock half way, and go on from there. It comes up again a few seconds later.
  • The rock island at the end is too high to climb onto from the water, so the crocodiles are the way across.

The stepping stones on each difficulty

  • Easy: they never go under while anyone is about, so timing does not matter. The gaps are about 1.3 m.
  • Normal: each holds 2.2 s from the first step, with 0.9 s of warning, then sinks. No harm done. Gaps about 2 m.
  • Hard: each holds 1.1 s, with 0.6 s of warning. Stay on one until its hold runs out and it snaps: a heart, and you are thrown off. Gaps about 2.6 m.
  • The bot crosses on every difficulty as each hero, with no heart lost.

Sounds, for your pick

Two new sounds for the rope are on the sound board, each with a second choice: taking hold (a short creak) and letting go (a whoosh). All four are recordings, CC0. Tell me A or B for each. The stepping stones use the crocodile sounds you already picked: its growl as its hold runs out, its bite if it snaps, and its splash, quieter, as one comes up or goes under.

What is not checked

  • I cannot watch this move. I checked it in still frames and with the rule tests. You are the first to see it at speed.
  • The heroes have no clip of their own for hanging on. They hang in the pose they have in the air, tipped to the line of the rope. Caputo hangs by his paws. If either looks wrong, say so.
  • Touch. The right thumb lets go, as it jumps. I have not held a finger on a real iPad.
  • The stepping-stone crocodiles are the lake’s crocodile, bigger. They float in its lurking pose and snap with its lunge. If they should look different from the ones that hunt, say so.
