What to listen for

  • Anything harsh, sharp or startling. The players are kids. The two I would check first are the marmot scout's whistle (the highest sound here) and the mosquito's whine.
  • Anything that jumps out as louder than the rest. Start the grove music, then go down the page pressing play. Every sound was brought to one loudness by measurement and then set against the others by its job: warnings, hits and checkpoints on top, what the hero does under them, footsteps under that, music under everything.
  • Whether the two warnings are easy to tell apart, from each other and from everything else. They are what make the enemies fair to dodge.
  • The grove music. Is it calm enough to hear for ten minutes, and does it sound like this forest? "Peaceful Forest" under Other choices is ready if not.
  • The golden leaf tune. I wrote it in code: seven bell notes climbing a bright chord. If it sounds thin beside the music, the short steel-drum "ta-da" under Other choices is the fallback, or tell me what it should feel like.

What you should know

  • I cannot hear any of this. I chose recordings by their descriptions, licenses and measurements (length, loudness, how bright, which key), and built the made-in-code sounds from pitches and shapes that are gentle by design. You are the first to hear them. Trust your ears over my descriptions.
  • Nothing came from the 2D game. Caputo Mountain has no sound or music files, only placeholder beeps in its fishing code.
  • Recorded: seven footsteps and the grove music, all CC0 from OpenGameArt. Made in code: everything else, so those add nothing to the download.
  • The mosquito's hovering whine is an extra, not on your list. It is how the mosquito gets heard before it is on screen. Say no and it goes.
  • Caputo's footsteps are Yaarbro's, made quieter, lighter and softer. There is no separate cat recording.
  • Size: 1.27 MB of sound files, against the 4 MB allowed.
