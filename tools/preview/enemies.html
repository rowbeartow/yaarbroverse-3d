What to look for

  • “Lurking”. Only its eyes and the top of its snout show above the water, as you asked. This is how it waits, and how a player first spots it.
  • “Lunge and bite”. The one that matters most. It gathers, throws its head forward with its jaws wide, and snaps them shut a third of a second in. In the game it will also shoot forward through the water; here it stays put, so you can see the bite.
  • “Surface swim” and “Underwater swim”. It drives itself with long sweeps of the tail, legs folded back along its sides, the way a real crocodile swims.
  • The tail is straight now. In Meshy’s model it was curled round to one side; I laid it out straight behind before giving it a skeleton.
  • “Beaten”. It rolls onto its side and sinks out of sight.

What is not perfect

  • The tell before a lunge is not in these clips. The eyes sinking and a wake heading for the hero are the game moving the crocodile, in the next step, not a clip.
  • The jaw opens like a hinged box. The model’s mouth is closed, so I cut the lower jaw free along the mouth line. The inside of the mouth is the same colour as the outside.
  • The legs only fold back. It never walks: it lives in the water. When it lunges they splay a little.
  • It is a little simpler than on the candidates page: 8 thousand triangles, down from 31 thousand, so several can swim at once without costing the lake its frames. The lake runs at 75 to 81 a second on your Mac, and must stay above 60.
  • Its size in the lake is set when it is placed. Here it is drawn to fit the picture.
  • I judged these from still frames in flat light. I cannot watch them move. You are the first to see them in motion, so trust your eye over my description.
