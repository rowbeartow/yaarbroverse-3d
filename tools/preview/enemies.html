Pick a clip under each picture. Drag a picture to turn it yourself. A clip that plays once rests on its last pose for a moment, then plays again.

What to look for

  • Marmot, “Acorn ready” then “Throw”. These are the two that matter most: the warning has to read at a glance as “something is about to be thrown at me”. It rears up on its hind legs with its right paw back over its shoulder, then whips the arm forward and drops onto all fours.
  • Marmot, “Winded”. This is the opening to hit it or get past, so it should look plainly different from “On watch”: head hung, back rounded, panting.
  • Mosquito, “Hover”. Wings beating 12 times a second, body bobbing, the abdomen and the five legs swinging late and loose.
  • Mosquito, “Dive ready” then “Dive”. It draws its legs up and cocks its abdomen with the wings racing, then goes beak first with the wings swept back. The game points the whole mosquito along the line of its dive, so here it stays level.
  • The wings. They are now pale and see-through on both sides. The dark green underside from the candidates page is gone.

What is not perfect

  • The marmot’s paw is empty. There is no acorn in its raised paw in “Acorn ready”. The game draws the acorn, at the paw and then in flight, when the enemies go into the level.
  • The mosquito has five legs. Meshy made its two middle legs as one leg under the middle of the body. It hangs and swings like the others.
  • “Beaten” is only the start of each defeat. The game moves and fades the enemy while the clip plays. Here the marmot lies on its back and the mosquito hangs upside down in the air.
  • The marmot’s harness bends with its body. It is part of its skin, so it stretches a little when it rears up.
  • Both are a little simpler than on the candidates page. Meshy’s models have about 31 thousand triangles each, over twice what a hero has. These have 14 thousand, so three enemies do not cost the Mac its 60 frames a second. In still frames I cannot tell them from the full ones. If you can, say so.
  • Both are drawn the same width here. Their sizes in the level are set when they are placed.
  • I judged these from still frames in flat light. I cannot watch them move. You are the first to see them in motion, so trust your eye over my description.
