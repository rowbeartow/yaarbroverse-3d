The fish, moving

The kids’ picks (bass A, perch A, trout B, pike A), each on the same simple skeleton with the same four clips. These are the files the game loads, in the game’s light.

Loading the models…

Pick a clip under the picture. Drag a picture to turn it yourself. A clip that plays once rests on its last pose for a moment, then plays again.

What to look for

  • “Swimming”. A wave runs down the body and grows toward the tail fin, and the head swings a little the other way, as a real fish swims.
  • “Thrashing”. The one that matters most in play: it is the tell to stop reeling. It should read as a fight from across the water.
  • “Landed”. On its side on the boards, two slaps of the tail and a rest.
  • The pike. The monster pike will be this model, much bigger and darker.

What is not perfect

  • The fins have no bones of their own. Each follows the part of the body it grows from. That is the “simple” in simple rig.
  • No mouth opens. The models’ mouths are closed. The monster pike needs jaws that snap, so its jaw is cut free in part two, the way the crocodile’s was.
  • They are simpler than on the candidates page: 3 thousand triangles each (the pike 5 thousand), down from 31 thousand, so several can be in the water at the dock, the lake’s heaviest place.
  • I judged these from still frames in flat light. I cannot watch them move. Trust your eye over my description.

Your call
