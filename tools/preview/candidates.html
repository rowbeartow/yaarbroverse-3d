What I see in them

  • The art converted well. Both studies came out as recognisable 3D characters on the first try, so I do not need a different reference picture.
  • Marmot A: the cleanest shape. Four separate legs in a wide stance, strap and acorn pouch in place. Its hind feet splay out behind it.
  • Marmot B: the face nearest the 2D scout, with the heavy angry brows. The strap came out as a harness that also runs over its rump, which the picture does not have, and the fur is spikier.
  • Marmot C: close to A, a little narrower, hind legs tucked under. Brows between A and B.
  • Mosquito A: pale wings on both sides, the nearest to the picture, but the two wings sit at different angles. Thinnest legs.
  • Mosquito B: the largest wings and the fattest striped body. Its eyes read as plain yellow from the side.
  • Mosquito C: the angriest face from the front, even wings, tidy legs.
  • On B and C the underside of the wings came out dark green. That is paint, not shape. Whichever you pick, I make the wings pale and see-through when I rig it, so do not let wing colour decide.
  • If you want my pick: marmot A and mosquito C, because they are the simplest to rig cleanly. Marmot B if the face matters more to you than the extra strap.
  • I judged these from still pictures in flat light. This session has no graphics card. You are the first to see them turning in the full light.
