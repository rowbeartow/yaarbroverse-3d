The walk, from the side, slowed down

The pale stones are set in the ground, which moves at the speed the game moves him. A paw that is down should stay beside its stone. For smoothness, choose Tenth speed and watch a paw leave the ground and come down again, and watch the moment the slider jumps from the right end back to the left: that is where the loop restarts, and the left hind paw lands there.

The trot, from the side, slowed down

Two beats to a stride. Left hind and right front land together, then right hind and left front. Between beats there is a short moment with no paw down. The back should stay level, the head steady and the tail up.

The run, from the side, slowed down

Four beats to a stride, one paw at a time, going round the body: left hind, right hind, right front, left front. To check, press Pause and drag the slider. The paws land with the slider at about 19, 32, 76 and 89 hundredths of the way along. Between the second and third he is off the ground, stretched long. The left paws are the ones on the far side of him here; Show skeleton makes them easier to follow.

What you asked for, and where it stands

  • TrotNew. Diagonal pairs land together, left hind with right front, then right hind with left front, two even beats to a stride of 0.4 seconds. Each paw is down for 44 hundredths of the stride, so there is a short float between beats. The back stays level and only bobs about a centimetre, the head takes under a tenth of the body’s movement, and the tail is carried up with a small sway.
  • RunRewritten as a rotary gallop. Counting from the left hind paw landing, the right hind lands 13 hundredths of a stride later, the right front at 57 and the left front at 70. That is about 60 thousandths of a second between the two paws of a pair, and each paw is down for about a quarter of the stride. He is off the ground twice: for a fifth of the stride, stretched long, after the hind legs push, and for a moment, gathered, after the front legs push. The spine still curls and stretches. These times are what the script writes into the clip, and I stepped through a stride in still frames to check them. I have not watched it at speed.
  • WalkThe same walk, with three causes of roughness removed. One: each paw’s upward speed jumped from nothing to over a metre a second in the instant it left the ground. Two: each paw was braking at its hardest in the instant it touched down, then stopped dead. Three: the front legs were 99 hundredths straight as the paw left, where the smallest change snaps the elbow. Now every paw eases off the ground and onto it, the paw’s tilt is one flowing curve where it used to stop and start four times a stride, and the front paw peels off earlier so the leg is never pulled straight. The sudden change in how fast the elbow turns is about a seventh of what it was. The loop restarts on exactly the same pose it ends on, and the left hind paw that lands there now lands softly, which was the hitch.
  • Changing gaitHe walks up to 34 hundredths of top speed (about 2 metres a second), trots up to 68 hundredths (about 4 metres a second), and gallops above that. Coming down he changes a little later, at 63 and 30, so a thumb held near a change does not flick him between two gaits. All three clips run on one clock and never drift apart, so a change carries on from the same point of the stride. The change itself takes about a tenth of a second.
  • JumpUnchanged. No pause before take-off: he leaves the ground the instant Jump is pressed.

What I measured, and what is not perfect

  • In a steady gait the paws hold the ground. At every speed I tried, in all three gaits, a paw that is down moves about half a centimetre against the ground for each metre he travels.
  • At a change of gait the paws do slide a little, once. The gaits put the paws down in different orders, so for the tenth of a second that two are mixed a planted paw moves about 10 centimetres at walk to trot, and about 15 to 20 at trot to gallop, while he covers a metre or more. Real cats also scramble for a stride there. If it shows when you watch “In the game”, tell me.
  • On the keyboard you will mostly see the gallop. He is at top speed almost at once. The walk and the trot show on the iPad with a gentle push of the thumb.
  • One clip per gait. A real cat lengthens its stride as it speeds up. Here each gait is one clip played slower or faster, so the trot is quick-stepping at the top of its range and the gallop is a little floaty at the bottom of its range.
  • I judged this from still frames and numbers. I cannot watch it move at speed. You are the first to see it in motion.
