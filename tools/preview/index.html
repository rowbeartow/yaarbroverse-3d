What you asked for, and where it stands

  • WalkOne paw at a time: left hind, left front, right hind, right front. The script works out the hind paws’ path from the front paws’, so each hind paw comes down in the print the front paw on its side has just left. Two new shoulder-blade bones lift the skin over each shoulder by about 6 cm while that leg carries weight. The head is held level and takes a tenth of the chest’s movement. The hips swing 5 degrees and the shoulders 4, so the spine bends from side to side. Each front paw curls back at the wrist as it lifts, reaches with the toes up, and has no downward speed left when it touches.
  • RunA bounding gallop. The hind paws land a sixteenth of a stride apart and push, he is in the air stretched long for a fifth of the stride, the front paws land one after the other, and the back curls as the legs gather. The head takes under a third of the body’s movement.
  • IdleAn 8 second loop: two slow breaths, weight easing left then right, a look to his left and a shorter look to his right, three ear flicks on two new ear bones, and four flicks of the tail tip.
  • JumpCrouch with the hind legs gathered, push from the hind legs with the front paws reaching, then fully stretched. See the first limit below.
  • FallingLegs down and spread, back a little rounded, tail out behind and swinging. It moves, where before it was a held pose.
  • LandingFront paws first, hind paws next, the body sinks about 25 cm, then he stands.
  • TailEach tail bone copies the one before it a moment late, so a movement of the hips runs down the tail and the tip swings widest. It uncurls and streams out in the run, the jump and the fall.

Where a script falls short

  • The jump’s crouch is a flash. The rules lift him the moment Jump is pressed, so the crouch and push play in a tenth of a second. A crouch you can really see needs a short pause before he leaves the ground. That is a change to how the game plays, not to how he looks, so I left it for you to decide.
  • You will rarely see the stroll. On the keyboard he is at full speed almost at once, which is the gallop. The walk shows on the iPad with a gentle push, and the game plays it up to twice as fast as the side view above before the run takes over.
  • The shoulder blades are subtle on him. He has a thick ruff and short legs. They show best from behind and a little above, on the stage, with Walk and Slow motion on.
  • I judged this from still frames. I cannot watch it move at speed. You are the first to see it in motion.
  • What a script does not give you: skin sliding over muscle, toes spreading under weight, and the small timing choices an animator makes by eye. If the walk on this page still does not read as a cat to you, more script rounds are unlikely to close that gap, and an animator is the right next step.
