# Yaarbroverse 3D

## What this is

A 3D browser game set in the Yaarbroverse, the sibling of the 2D game Caputo Mountain. Made for two kids: the older one finds games too easy, the younger one finds them too hard. They play on an iPad (touch, held sideways) and a Mac (keyboard). The owner is not a developer: make the technical calls, explain anything he has to do as numbered steps with the exact link to open.

Read first: `docs/session-handoff.md` (what exists, what's next, open questions), then `docs/decisions.md` (settled; do not reopen).

## Status

In development. Two playable areas. The Bouncing Grove: a full level in six sections that ends with a boss, the marmot chief. It has three enemies on the way (tougher on Normal and Hard), pieces that move (tipping logs, crumbling moss, windy boughs), hearts, a move for each hero, and sound and music. Cocolalla Lake (reshaped in milestone nine): eight sections that wind round a wooded island and across the water, with a camera that turns to follow the route: a shore path, crocodile stepping stones up the east channel, two rope swings across a cove to a headland, sunken logs, a long swim back toward the camera, a dive through a rocky point, old pier posts and a boardwalk to the fishing dock; both heroes swim and dive, with a dive hint and the controls on the level card and pause screen. Around them: up to four players on a device, a level picker, saved best times and a ghost of each player's best run to race. The lake has its own music and the sound of lapping water, and crocodiles in its long swim (water enemies: they reach a swimmer underwater, and on Normal and Hard they hunt a swimmer anywhere in the open water). The lake must stay at 60 frames a second or more on the Mac (75 lowest on Hard before it was reshaped). Milestone ten made the lake worth playing (the owner's OK on the plan, 2026-11-17): underwater rebuilt; huckleberries in trails through every section and three hidden leaves, saved per player; logs and posts that sink; marmot scouts, giant mosquitoes and more crocodiles (3, 8 and 12 enemies), each kept off the trickiest jumps; and the Open Water rebuilt with two log booms, weed beds and a rest rock. Milestone eleven, part one, gave the lake headroom (the owner measured it at exactly 60 on Hard): its mosquitoes are drawn from a lighter model and only the six nearest enemies within 45 m are drawn; the lake has fishing from a jetty off the fishing dock (bass, perch, trout and pike; no timing needed on Easy, and on Hard the line snaps), with its own sounds; Yaarbro casts, reels and holds a landed fish up on his palms; Caputo holds the rod in his jaws, flings a landed fish onto the boards and stands over it; and there is a fish book for each player (how many of each kind, and the biggest) on the end card and the lake's card. The monster pike comes next (Walblum's kick for Yaarbro and a new ability for Caputo arrive with the pike), then the grove reshaped by the route rule; `docs/session-handoff.md` has the roadmap and what the owner has still to check on a real device.
Live at: https://rowbeartow.github.io/yaarbroverse-3d/ . Every merge to `main` publishes there, straight to the kids' devices.

## Architecture

- Framework: Three.js, TypeScript, Vite. No physics library.
- Assets: source textures, the forest light (HDRI) and models in `assets/`. `scripts/build-assets.mjs` compresses them into `public/media/` (KTX2 textures, meshopt geometry, a smaller HDR) before every build and before `npm run dev`. `public/media/` is not committed. Third-party files are CC0 and listed in `CREDITS.md`; the build fails over 20 MB. Recorded sounds and music are in `assets/audio/` (WAV and MP3, which Safari plays). Each sound is shared or belongs to named levels (`home` in `src/audio/sounds.ts`): shared sounds are fetched at start-up and must stay under 1 MB together, a level's own (its music, its enemies' sounds) are fetched when it is picked and must stay under 2.5 MB a level; `tests/sound-files.test.ts` checks both. A level's enemy models are fetched when it is picked too.
- Hero models: `assets/heroes/` holds them already compressed, and the build copies them as they are. The owner's originals (20 to 30 MB each, files on the `hero-models` release) are never committed. Blender scripts in `tools/`, run with no window, make each model from its originals: `prepare-rigged.py` for a character Meshy rigged (Yaarbro: puts his one-clip files on one model, makes each clip happen on the spot, cleans up the skin), `rig-quadruped.py` for one Meshy could not rig (Caputo: builds the skeleton, skins it, writes the clips), and `pose-test.py` to look at a skin in test poses. `tools/compress-hero.mjs` then makes the small file and lists its clips. `tools/preview/` builds the page the owner reviews a hero on before a change to it is merged. Enemy models are kept the same way in `assets/enemies/`: `rig-quadruped.py` rigs the marmot scout and the crocodile (each guide file picks the animal's own clips; the crocodile's also lays its curled tail out straight and gives it a jaw that opens), `rig-insect.py` rigs the giant mosquito, `joint-views.py` draws a model on a grid to find its joints, and `tools/preview/enemies.mjs` builds the page the owner reviews them on. `tools/meshy-image-to-3d.mjs` makes enemy model candidates from a picture through Meshy's API, and `tools/preview/candidates.mjs` builds the page the owner picks one on. `tools/meshy-picture-builds.mjs` makes candidates for several things in one style, a picture from words for each and then a model from each picture with each of two of Meshy's model makers (the fish: `tools/fish.candidates.json`). `tools/rig-fish.py` rigs any of the four fish with `tools/fish.rig.json` (one skeleton, four clips), and their compressed files are kept in `assets/fish/`. `tools/thin-model.mjs` makes a lighter copy of an enemy's model for a level whose frame budget is tight (the lake's mosquito, `mosquito-light.glb`). `tools/meshy-portrait-to-3d.mjs` does the same for a character whose portrait does not show all of them (Walblum): a full-body picture from the portrait first, then a model from each picture. `tools/meshy-rig-animate.mjs` rigs a character with Meshy's Rigging API and gives it clips from Meshy's animation library or Text to Motion (Yaarbro's swimming clips: `tools/yaarbro.swim.json`); `prepare-rigged.py` carries such clips onto his model bone by bone (`"retarget": true` in the guide), because the new rig stands a little differently.
- Deploy target: GitHub Pages, static files only. `.github/workflows/publish.yml` builds and publishes on every merge to main.
- Data layer: none. The browser's own storage on each device (local storage, and IndexedDB for ghosts) keeps the players (up to four, each a typed name, a colour, their last hero and difficulty, their bests, what they have collected, their fish book and the ghost of each best run) and whether sound is muted. Nothing about a player goes into the repo, a commit, a log or a network request.
- Auth: none.
- External services: none, and none are allowed inside the game (no accounts, payments, online services or AI calls).

How it fits together:

- `src/game/`: the rules. One hero in one level, advanced in fixed steps of 1/60 second. No browser or 3D code, so tests run it without a screen.
  - `ghost.ts`: records where a hero was during a run and gives it back at any moment, as small text for storage. Looked at only: the rules never see a ghost.
  - `boss.ts`: bosses. Any level can end with one: an arena that starts the fight, a loop of warning, attack and rest, stages that change its attacks as it loses health, sidekicks it can call, and a goal kept back until it is beaten. `docs/adding-a-level.md` says how to give a level one.
  - `route-shape.ts`: the route rule. Routes turn: it measures how much of a level's route runs away from the camera, across the screen and back toward it, and how often it turns; `tests/route-shape.test.ts` holds every level to the minimum. A section's `facing` says which way the camera faces there. `docs/adding-a-level.md` has the rule.
  - `assist.ts`: the jump assist (Easy and Normal): a jump aimed roughly at small footing or a rope steers onto it, for a keyboard's eight directions. The keyboard rule (`tests/keyboard.test.ts`) plays every level through eight keys relative to the camera; `docs/adding-a-level.md` has both.
  - `rope.ts`: rope swings any level can hang: a hero in the air takes hold, swings out and back, and lets go with Jump to fly on; with help (Easy) letting go anywhere lands safely. `docs/adding-a-level.md` says how to hang one.
  - `collect.ts`: things to collect, which any level can have: huckleberries in trails and a few hidden leaves. Touching one picks it up; a fall takes nothing away; they never block, hurt or help. `docs/adding-a-level.md` says how to lay them.
  - `fishing.ts`: fishing any level can have: a spot beside water with fish in it. One button and the stick: hold to slide a ring out, let go to cast, hook the bite, reel while the fish tires and let the line run while it thrashes, or the line snaps. `game.ts` steps it while the hero stands on the spot. `docs/adding-a-level.md` says how to give a level one.
  - `swim.ts`: water any level can have: pools a hero swims in (never a fall), diving, swimming up, breath, climbing out, beds of weed that slow a swimmer at the surface, and floating booms that cannot be climbed onto. `game.ts` steps it. No stretch of water may be a trap: `tests/water-exits.test.ts` drops a hero in all along every level's route and checks it gets out. `docs/adding-a-level.md` says how to use it.
  - `moving.ts`: pieces that move: a solid can tip, crumble, sway or sink (a log or post that goes under the water a moment after a hero lands, and comes back), and carries a hero standing on it; and the crocodile stepping stone, which lies low until a hero is near, holds from the first step on it, then goes under (and on Hard snaps). Each game keeps its own copy of a level's moving pieces. `docs/adding-a-level.md` says how to use them.
  - `enemy.ts`: the marmot scout, the giant mosquito and the crocodile: what each does, what hurts a hero and what strikes an enemy. `game.ts` steps them with the hero. A level's difficulty can make a scout walk a beat and give chase, and the mosquito keep up and dive more often.
  - `crocodile.ts`: the crocodile, the one enemy in the water: it lurks, swims after a hero at their depth, gives a tell (its eyes sink and it creeps along its aim), lunges, and rests. It is the only enemy that reaches a swimmer underwater. On Normal and Hard the lake's crocodiles hunt: all the open water is theirs, and they head for where a swimmer is going.
- `src/levels/index.ts`: the level list. The game builds whichever level is chosen from it, and the rule tests and the bot run over every level in it. `docs/adding-a-level.md` is the recipe for a new one.
- `src/levels/<level>/difficulty.ts`: every Easy, Normal and Hard number for that level, in one file. `layout.ts` next to it builds the level from those numbers. `level.ts` is what the level brings to the list: its name, layout, scenery and music. `src/levels/sandbox/` is a tiny development level, never shown to players; it ends with a practice boss that proves the boss system. `src/levels/cocolalla/` is Cocolalla Lake, the swimming level.
- `src/view/`: draws the level and hero and moves the camera. Looks only.
  - `grove-view.ts`: the renderer, lighting (forest light, sun with soft shadows, filmic tone mapping, glow), camera, and switching quality tiers.
  - `scenery.ts`: the level's solids as smooth textured shapes, and moving pieces put where the rules have them every frame (`placeMoving`). `forest.ts`: the gully, trees, ferns, grass, mist, light. `lake.ts`: a lake's surroundings (open water, lake bed, the shore round three sides, any wooded island, pines, reeds, the log booms along the pools' open edges, the Selkirks), and `lake-solids.ts` its boulders, crags and wooden deck. All build shapes with `surface.ts` and `noise.ts`.
  - `route.ts`: where the route runs (its middle, sides and height), which the forest is laid out around, and the rule for drawing only the stretches of scenery near the hero (no 3D code, tested).
  - `route-camera.ts`: the camera that follows the route: which way it faces for the section the hero is heading into, how it eases round, and turning the stick's directions into the level's so up is always away from the camera (no 3D code, tested).
  - `bank-outline.ts`: the edge of a bank's top. It may be irregular, but never reaches past where the rules let a hero stand (no 3D code, tested).
  - `materials.ts`: surfaces, plus three additions to the standard ones: mist by height, moss blending, wind sway. `pictures.ts`: textures drawn in code.
  - `assets.ts`: downloads `media/` and reports progress: textures, the light and the heroes at start-up, and each level's enemy models when it is picked (`modelFilesOf` in `enemies.ts` says which). `quality.ts`: the tiers and the rules for choosing one (no 3D code, tested).
  - `water.ts`: pools drawn (see-through surface with waves, a rock basin with light rippling over it, left out where the surroundings draw a lake bed), splashes, rings and bubbles; `grove-view.ts` does the underwater view (camera under the surface, blue-green fog).
  - `weed-beds.ts`: a pool's beds of weed drawn, every pad in a level in one draw.
  - `croc-stones.ts`: crocodile stepping stones drawn from the crocodile's model, each put where the rules have its back.
  - `fishing.ts`: fishing drawn: the marked spot, the ring, bobber, line and rod, and the fish from their models (`assets/fish/`), each with a dark shape on the water over it. Yaarbro has the rod in his palm with a reel under it, and a landed fish lies across his palms. Caputo has the rod in his jaws; he flings a landed fish over himself onto the boards behind and turns to stand over it.
  - `held-tackle.ts`: where the things a hero holds while fishing go: the rod, its reel and a landed fish, each at a grip point in the middle of a palm, because Yaarbro's model has no finger bones, or for Caputo at a grip point in his jaws, with where his flung fish and dropped rod lie on the boards (no drawing, tested). The game and the hero's preview page both place things with it, and `tools/preview/fishing-measure.mjs` measures every frame of it.
  - `pickups.ts`: huckleberries (every one in a level is one draw) and hidden leaves drawn, bobbing, and popping when picked up.
  - `ropes.ts`: rope swings drawn (the rope, the snag and bough that hold it), and a hero hung from a rope's end.
  - `sight.ts`: which enemies are drawn this frame: those the mist does not hide, or on a level with `enemySight` (the lake) the nearest few within a distance (no 3D code, tested).
  - `enemies.ts`: draws each enemy from its model (a level with `lightEnemies` uses the lighter copy where there is one), the acorn, the line an attack is aimed along, and a crocodile's wake. `boss.ts`: draws a boss from an enemy's model, bigger and recoloured, with its acorns and aim lines.
  - `heroes.ts`: builds a hero from its model (`hero-model.ts`, mixing rules in `hero-motion.ts`). To give a character a model, see the top of `hero-model.ts`.
- `src/audio/`: sound. Nothing plays before the first tap or key press.
  - `sounds.ts`: every sound and the music in one list: what it is for, its file in `assets/audio/` (or its recipe), its group, its level within the group, and the levels it belongs to (`home`; left out, it is shared). `MIX` at the top is how loud each group and the whole game is, in decibels: the six numbers the owner sets on the mixer page.
  - `mix.ts`: whether sound may play yet, mute and its memory (no browser code, tested). `game-sounds.ts`: turns what the rules report into sounds: events, footsteps by surface and hero, the roll, the mosquito's whine, and fading with distance (no browser code, tested).
  - `graph.ts`: how the sounds are wired: each sound, its group's volume, the master, the underwater muffle, the top end turned down, a soft limiter. Shared by the game, the mixer page and the measuring tool. `player.ts`: the game's speaker: fetches the shared files at start-up and a level's own when it is picked (letting the last level's go), decodes them and plays them through that wiring.
  - `synth.ts`: the recipes for the few sounds made in code, and the step that brings short sounds to one loudness.
- `src/save/ghosts.ts`: each player's ghosts, one entry for each level, hero and difficulty, apart from their progress. `src/save/ghost-store.ts` keeps them in the browser's database (IndexedDB), carries over ghosts an earlier version left in local storage, and falls back to local storage where there is no database.
- `src/save/players.ts`: the players on this device and their saved progress: bests, and what each has collected on each level (the most huckleberries in a run and the hidden leaves found, by difficulty, and every huckleberry ever picked), and their fish book (for each kind of fish, how many they have landed and the biggest). Reading and writing never throw: damaged saved data is dropped and the game carries on (no browser code, tested).
- `src/input/controls.ts`: keyboard and touch, turned into "which way", "is Jump held" and "is the move button held" (Yaarbro's roll, Caputo's pounce).
- `src/ui/fishing-words.ts`: what the game says while fishing and what the round button is called at each moment (no browser code).
- `src/ui/screens.ts` and `index.html`: start screen, pause, end card, "turn sideways" card, messages, hearts and a boss's health bar. `src/ui/who.ts`: the "Who's playing?" screen, shown first, with the grown-ups' reset behind a confirmation. `src/ui/picker.ts`: the level picker on the start screen, a card for each area with the current player's finished difficulties and best times, and the ghost switch.
- `src/main.ts`: the loop that connects them, the Sound button and the M key. The start screen shows first; the 3D code and files load behind it with a progress bar.

Address-bar switches: `?fps` shows the frame rate and tier, `?quality=high|medium|low|minimal` fixes the tier, `?level=sandbox` opens the development level, `?standin` plays both heroes as the CC0 stand-in model (for checking the loader and animation player against a library file).

## Run and deploy

Node 22 (`.nvmrc`).

- Install: `npm ci`
- Run locally: `npm run dev`, then open the address it prints
- Test the rules: `npm test` (fast, no browser). It includes the keyboard rule: the bot plays every level on Easy and Normal through eight keys (`tests/helpers/keyboard.ts`). The rule tests come in two kinds: the bot's runs (any test file that uses a helper that plays the game: `tests/bot-runs.ts` works out which) and the rest. `npm test` runs both; `npm run test:rules` runs the quick ones and `npm run test:bot` the bot's runs. A test in a bot run may take five minutes, so never give one a time limit of its own. `GROVE_TIMES=1 npx vitest run tests/sections.test.ts --silent=false` prints how long the bot's perfect run takes to the end of each section.
- Build: `npm run build` (prepares the assets, and fails if the download goes over 20 MB)
- Start-up, measured: with the built game served, `node tools/startup-measure.mjs` prints what a visit downloads before Play works and how long it takes on a 20 megabit connection (the top of that file says the rest).
- What a frame costs: with the built game served, `node tools/draw-measure.mjs [level] [difficulty]` counts draw calls, triangles and enemies drawn at places along a level on the high tier. Count the lake on Hard before and after any change to what it draws.
- Pictures for the docs: `node scripts/screenshot.mjs` (see the top of that file)
- Rebuild Yaarbro from his Meshy files: `blender -b -P tools/prepare-rigged.py -- <folder of Meshy files> <out.glb> tools/yaarbro.guide.json`, then compress `<out.glb>`. The folder needs his six files from the `hero-models` release, the four swimming files in `tools/yaarbro-swim-clips/` (see its README) and the three rope files in `tools/yaarbro-rope-clips/` (made by `tools/yaarbro.rope.json`, stripped by `tools/strip-clip.mjs`). His three fishing clips are made by the script itself from the guide's keys, with what he holds at a grip point in each palm; `node tools/preview/fishing-measure.mjs` must pass before a change to them, to his grips or to `src/view/held-tackle.ts` merges.
- Make swimming clips (or other clips) with Meshy: `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1 node tools/meshy-rig-animate.mjs tools/yaarbro.swim.json <scratch folder>` (about 27 credits; it prints the balance before and after and stops over `MESHY_MAX_CREDITS`, default 100).
- Rig Caputo from his Meshy file: `blender -b -P tools/rig-quadruped.py -- <caputo original.glb> <out.glb> tools/caputo.rig.json`, then compress `<out.glb>`. The script writes his three fishing clips too (the rod in his jaws; `"grips"` in the guide says where, and his entry in `HERO_MODELS` must say the same); `node tools/preview/fishing-measure.mjs caputo` must pass before a change to them merges.
- Rig the marmot scout: `blender -b -P tools/rig-quadruped.py -- <marmot original.glb> <out.glb> tools/marmot.rig.json`. Rig the giant mosquito: `blender -b -P tools/rig-insect.py -- <mosquito original.glb> <out.glb> tools/mosquito.rig.json`. Rig the crocodile (from `crocodile-c.glb` on the `hero-models` release, never made again): `blender -b -P tools/rig-quadruped.py -- <crocodile-c.glb> <out.glb> tools/crocodile.rig.json`. Then `node tools/compress-hero.mjs <out.glb> <marmot, mosquito or crocodile> 1024 enemies`.
- Rig a fish: `blender -b -P tools/rig-fish.py -- <picked original.glb> <out.glb> tools/fish.rig.json <bass, perch, trout or pike>`, then `node tools/compress-hero.mjs <out.glb> <name> 512 fish`.
- Find a model's joints for a guide file: `blender -b -P tools/joint-views.py -- <model.glb> <out.png> [guide.json]` (also needs `apt-get install -y python3-pil`).
- All of these need Blender 4 with numpy. In a cloud session run `apt-get update && apt-get install -y blender python3-numpy` once (Blender is not always there already). The steps are at the top of each script.
- Compress a hero model: `node tools/compress-hero.mjs <model.glb> <name>` (see the top of that file)
- Hero preview page: `npm run assets`, then `node tools/preview/build.mjs <rigged caputo.glb> <folder>` (see the top of that file, which also says how to publish it). Publish the folder as a private Claude artifact and wait for the owner's OK before merging a change to a hero. `node tools/preview/stills.mjs` takes still frames of the page, for judging a clip in a session that cannot watch it move, and `node tools/preview/measure.mjs` measures how far planted paws slide in each gait and at each change of gait.
- Enemy preview page: `node tools/preview/enemies.mjs <folder> marmot=<rigged.glb> mosquito=<rigged.glb> crocodile=<rigged.glb>` (any of them, or a fish: `bass=`, `perch=`, `trout=`, `pike=`), published as a private Claude artifact for the owner's OK before a change to an enemy merges. `node tools/preview/enemy-stills.mjs` takes still frames of it. With a hero's fishing clips on the page (`yaarbro=<rigged.glb>` and `held-bass=<the rigged bass>` and the other three fish), `node tools/preview/fishing-measure.mjs [yaarbro or caputo]` checks every frame of them: what he holds stays at the grip points in his palms (or, for Caputo, in his jaws), and nothing passes through his body or head.
- Lake preview page (the game itself, opening on any of several places on Cocolalla Lake (the sinking logs first), with a panel to pick the difficulty, hero and whether the test bot plays): `npm run assets`, then `node tools/preview/lake.mjs <folder>`, published as a private Claude artifact for the owner's OK before a change to the lake's pieces merges. `node tools/preview/lake-stills.mjs` takes still frames of it, holding the game still at each moment. The tops of those files say the rest. The page reaches the game through `window.__grovePreview` (`src/main.ts`): a place to start on, and a bot at the controls.
- Arena preview page (enemies and bosses at full speed, on the game's own rules, in a blocked-out level): `npm run assets`, then `node tools/preview/arena.mjs <folder> marmot=<rigged.glb> mosquito=<rigged.glb> crocodile=<rigged.glb> yaarbro=<rigged.glb> caputo=<rigged.glb>`, published as a private Claude artifact for the owner's OK before a change to how an enemy behaves merges. `node tools/preview/arena-stills.mjs` takes still frames of it. The tops of those files say the rest.
- Enemy model candidates: `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1 node tools/meshy-image-to-3d.mjs <picture.png> <scratch folder> <name>` asks Meshy for three models from one picture (30 credits each; it prints the balance before and after, and never prints a key). Then `node tools/preview/candidates.mjs <scratch folder> <page folder>` builds the page the owner picks on, and `node tools/preview/candidate-stills.mjs` takes a still of it. The tops of those files say the rest.
- Pictures of the screens ("Who's playing?", the level picker, a level card), for a preview page before a change to them merges: with the built game served, `node tools/preview/screen-shots.mjs <folder> [width] [height]`.
- Sound board: `node tools/preview/sounds.mjs <folder> [<extras folder>]` builds the page the owner listens to every sound on, published as a private Claude artifact for his OK before a sound goes into the game or changes. The top of that file says the rest.
- Mixer page: `node tools/preview/mixer.mjs <folder>` builds the page the owner sets the six mix levels on by ear, published as a private Claude artifact. With it served (`npx vite preview --outDir <folder> --port 4178`), `node tools/preview/mix-measure.mjs` measures the mix in LUFS (needs ffmpeg). The aim is about -23 LUFS in normal play with the music at least 10 decibels under the effects.
- Browser checks: `npm run test:browser` (builds, serves, then drives the game with keyboard and touch, in Chromium and in WebKit). In a cloud session Chromium is already installed: do not install it again. WebKit is not: run `npx playwright install --with-deps webkit` once per session. To run one browser: `npx playwright test --project=ipad-webkit` (or `mac`, `ipad`).
- Everything: `npm run check`, then `npm run test:browser`
- Deploy: merging to `main` publishes automatically. Do not deploy by hand. The same checks run on every pull request (`.github/workflows/checks.yml`). So does the private-terms check (`scripts/check-private-terms.mjs`); to try it locally: `PRIVATE_TERMS="one,two" node scripts/check-private-terms.mjs`.

## Conventions

- World, characters and tone come from Caputo Mountain (the owner's private `digglesworth` repo: its `docs/` folder and art) and from the owner's prompts. Anything the owner states in a prompt is canon; record it in `docs/decisions.md`. The private repo is read only; never change it. Do not invent canon. If something is needed that neither source gives, ask the owner.
- This repo is public. No family names, no files copied from the private repo. The one exception is the hero models the owner made from that art (`assets/heroes/`, compressed, all rights reserved; see `docs/decisions.md`).
- A check on every pull request fails if a private term (the repository secret `PRIVATE_TERMS`) appears in a tracked file, the pull request's title or description, or a commit message. The kids are "the younger player" and "the older player" everywhere. Player names in tests and docs are made up (Pip, Moss). If a flagged line is an ordinary word, reword it; never add an allowlist.
- Every level has Easy, Normal and Hard, with all its numbers in that level's `difficulty.ts`. After changing numbers run `npm test`: a bot plays each difficulty start to finish.
- Keep rules out of `src/view` and drawing out of `src/game`.
- Small pull requests with plain-words descriptions: what changed, how it was tested. Claude merges its own once the checks pass (see `docs/decisions.md`).
- Standing permission (the owner's, 2026-11-14): while one pull request's checks run, push the next one's work to a backup branch named `claude/backup-<topic>`, so no work exists only on the session's machine. Each report says which backup branches can be deleted.
- Backlog lives in GitHub Issues. New ideas become a one-line issue labeled `inbox`.
- Record settled decisions in `docs/decisions.md`. Append only.
- Keep `docs/session-handoff.md` true: what exists now, not plans written as if done.

Never modify or commit a file unrelated to the current task, including to produce a clean working tree.

## Known constraints

- The test browsers have no graphics card. The game notices (Chromium says so; for WebKit the checks add `?quality=minimal`) and uses the lowest tier: plain shading, no shadows, no smoothed edges, sparse trees, about 9 to 15 frames a second here and fewer on GitHub's machines. Browser checks run one at a time for that reason. The full lighting is checked on purpose by `tests/browser/look.spec.ts` at medium and high quality in a small window, which takes seconds a frame. Real devices are not affected.
- Browser checks must not wait on the clock for the game to do something. A slow machine draws few frames, and the game only moves forward a tenth of a second per frame. Wait on the game's own state (`expectAtRest` and `frames` in `tests/browser/helpers.ts`).
- Pictures drawn in code (`pictures.ts`) ask for `willReadFrequently`, which keeps the drawing on the processor. Without it a browser with no graphics card takes about 7 seconds to start.
- The Poly Haven files come straight from Poly Haven (`CREDITS.md` gives the addresses and checksums). A cloud session can only fetch them if its environment's network settings allow `polyhaven.com`, `api.polyhaven.com` and `dl.polyhaven.org`.
- Browser checks run in Chromium (Mac-sized with a keyboard, iPad-sized with touch) and in WebKit (iPad-sized with touch). On GitHub (`checks.yml`) the bot's runs have a job of their own ("Whole-level bot runs", an hour to finish in), so a slow machine cannot fail a run by being slow; the game is built once by the rule-tests-and-build job, which runs the quick rule tests, and handed to the browser jobs, which are split into shards that run side by side: six for Chromium, four for WebKit (`--shard`, with `fullyParallel` so any check can go to any shard; `PREBUILT=1` makes `playwright.config.ts` serve the handed-over build as it is). Two summary jobs keep the names branch protection requires. Every job runs inside Playwright's container image (`mcr.microsoft.com/playwright`), pinned to the exact Playwright version in `package.json`, so no run installs anything from Ubuntu's servers. To upgrade Playwright, change `package.json`, `package-lock.json` and every image line in `checks.yml` together; `scripts/check-playwright-image.mjs` fails the checks if they differ. WebKit is Safari's engine built for Linux, not Safari itself: Safari on a real iPad is only checked by hand.
- Nobody can hear a test browser, and a cloud session cannot hear at all. The checks prove sound is asked for at the right moments, is silent before the first tap and when muted, and raises no error (`tests/sound.test.ts`, `tests/browser/sound.spec.ts`, and `__grove.sound` on the page). How it sounds is judged by the owner, on the sound board and in the game.
- Sound must start from inside a tap or key press, or Safari stays silent. The page listens for every tap and key before anything else does (`main.ts`) and wakes the sound each time. Sound files are WAV for short sounds (nothing added at either end) and MP3 for music.
- The test tools can tap in WebKit but cannot hold a finger down or drag one. In WebKit, held and dragged fingers are touch pointer events handed to the element under the finger (see `tests/browser/touch.spec.ts`); in Chromium they are real touches.
